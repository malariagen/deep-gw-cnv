Experiment 43 results summary:
- 18_genome_cnv_caller eliminated the masked CNV pool: masked rate dropped from 12–33% to
  ≈0% across all genes; total FNR converged to evaluable FNR (CRT=0.02, GCH1=0.15, MDR1=0.00,
  PM2_PM3=0.02).
- β=4 improved HMM stability substantially (transitions p50: 306 → 127, 57% further reduction)
  with no posterior collapse (callability 0.995). But β=4 did NOT improve CRR contrast for
  GCH1 FNs: p50 moved 1.43 → 1.42, essentially unchanged.
- 306 GCH1 FNs remain (FNR=0.15): all have frac=0 (HMM gives no CN>=2 coverage) and CRR p50=1.42
  — below the 1.55 rescue threshold and unreachable by parameter tuning alone.

My diagnosis:
The 306 GCH1 FNs are a training data composition problem. GCH1 amplification is extremely
common in African Pf9 populations (AF-E FNR=0.39, AF-W FNR=0.34), which dominate the dataset.
With cnv_downsample_ratio_final=0.30, the VAE still sees enough amplified GCH1 profiles in
late training that it learns elevated GCH1 read counts as near-normal — keeping reconstruction
residual low and the HMM uninformed. β=4 added KL regularisation but cannot fix the fact that
the model has been shown too many amplified profiles: the normal-vs-amplified boundary remains
blurry for GCH1. Lowering the steady-state CNV proportion forces the model to treat amplification
as genuinely anomalous.

Proposed experiment 44: lower cnv_downsample_ratio_final 0.30 → 0.10
Changes from exp 43:
- cnv_downsample_ratio_final: 0.30 → 0.10 (full retrain required)
  At 10%, amplified profiles are rare enough in late training that the VAE reconstructs them
  poorly, raising the reconstruction residual and giving the HMM a signal to segment them CN>=2.
  All other parameters unchanged (β=4, 18_genome_cnv_caller, rescue threshold 1.55).

What I expect:
GCH1 frac=0 FNs should gain non-zero frac as the HMM starts seeing real reconstruction residual.
If enough cross the 0.50 gene-coverage threshold, GCH1 FNR could drop toward 0.05–0.10.
The main risk is that 10% is too low and the VAE becomes noisy for all samples, raising
transitions p50 back above 200+. Key diagnostic: if transitions p50 climbs above 250 without
a corresponding GCH1 FNR improvement, the ratio is too aggressive (next step: 0.15 or 0.20).

What we could do instead:
1. Intermediate ratio (0.15 or 0.20): more conservative step between 0.30 and 0.10; safer but
   likely insufficient if African GCH1 prevalence is ~40-60% (same composition problem persists).
2. Exclude known-CNV samples from the normal training pool entirely: train only on samples called
   normal in a prior experiment, so the VAE never sees amplified profiles as normal. Harder to
   implement but addresses the root cause more directly.

Run time estimate: ~7–8 hours (VAE training ~5–6h + HMM ~2h + CNV calling ~30 min + evaluation ~5 min).
